Why a true slab leak is the less common case in Draper

Slab leaks belong mostly to slab-on-grade houses, where supply lines run in or under the concrete. Draper homes are mostly built over basements and stem walls, so plumbing usually runs in joists and walls where leaks show sooner. We would rather say that plainly than sell you a diagnosis that does not fit.

Slabs still exist here. Garage floors, room additions, a basement slab with lines beneath it, and a few slab-on-grade houses can all hide pipe under concrete. A pinhole in a copper line or a failed joint leaks into the gravel bed, then the water finds a crack, a wall base or an expansion joint and comes up.

If the water is in a finished basement from a more ordinary cause, start with our flood damage restoration page or our water mitigation page. They cover the common cases.

Slab leak safety steps while water runs under the floor

Close the main

Shut off the main water valve. With the supply off, the leak stops feeding the floor.

Cut power to wet areas

If outlets or baseboard wiring are wet, turn off that circuit at a dry panel. If you cannot reach it safely, call the utility.

Call the plumber and us

A plumber locates and repairs the line. Call (385) 988-9410 once the plumber has the line under control and we will start on the floor.

Protect and photograph

Lift rugs and furniture off damp flooring and take dated photos of the stains and the water meter.

A gas smell means leave the building and call the gas utility from outside. Do not try to find a slab leak by listening near gas appliances.

Drying after the plumber breaks out the slab

1

Map the moisture

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show where water spread under flooring and into wall bases.

2

Extract standing water

Portable extraction units remove water from carpet, pad and low spots.

3

Open what stays wet

Baseboards and the bottom of walls may come off so air reaches the cavity.

4

Dry with equipment

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until readings match an unaffected area.

5

Clean and rebuild

Antimicrobial treatment goes on affected surfaces, then flooring, trim and paint finish the interior scope.

Concrete holds water and releases it slowly, so slab jobs often dry slower than wood. We follow readings rather than a calendar and use ANSI/IICRC S500 as a reference many professionals work from. We do not repair pipes or cut concrete. That is the plumber's job.

Groundwater, leaks and what a policy may cover

Water under a floor is not always from a pipe. The Utah Geological Survey says shallow groundwater can flood basements and other low-lying structures, and that standard homeowners insurance does not cover groundwater flooding. A plumber's test can show whether pressurized water is the source.

A sudden pipe failure is often treated differently from groundwater seepage or slow deterioration. Ask your insurer which category applies, and whether tear-out of the slab is part of any claim. No one here can promise what the policy will pay.

Mistakes to avoid with a suspected slab leak

  • Ignoring a high water bill because nothing looks wet.
  • Covering a damp floor with new flooring before the slab has been checked.
  • Digging or cutting the slab yourself without a Blue Stakes notice first.
  • Leaving the main on while you wait for a plumber.

Digging outside to reach a slab line

Some repairs mean breaking out concrete or digging beside the foundation. Blue Stakes of Utah says notice must be given at least three business days before excavation, and that the party actually doing the digging makes the call, no matter how deep. A plumber normally handles this, so ask when the locate is requested.

Can you find the leak under my slab?

No. A plumber locates and repairs the line. We handle the water that came up and the drying that follows.

Are slab leaks common in Draper?

Not very. Most homes here have basements and stem walls, so true slab leaks tend to involve garages, additions or slab-on-grade houses.

Can the floor be dried before the pipe is fixed?

Drying can begin once the water is shut off. Leaving the leak running just rewets the floor.
