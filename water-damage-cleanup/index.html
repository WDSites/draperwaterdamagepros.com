After the water stops, the cleanup begins

The pipe is fixed, the flood has drained, and the house still smells, the floor is gritty and the walls feel cold. This is the cleanup stage, and the first 48 hours matter. Emergency water damage cleanup in Draper starts with the same 24/7 dispatch line as every other job we run.

Cleanup is its own intent, separate from the drying work on our water mitigation page and the rebuilding on our water damage repair page. It is the removal and sanitizing between the two.

When cleanup is an emergency

Water that came from a toilet, a drain or the ground carries contamination, and the longer it sits the harder it is to clean. If sewage is involved, treat the room as unsafe until it has been cleaned by professionals.

Safety before cleanup

If water has reached outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, stay out and call the utility or 911 before calling us. Turn off power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach. Keep children and pets out of any water that came from a drain.

When it is safe, the useful first steps are to stop the source, lift valuables off the floor, open windows if the air outside is dry, and take photos before anything is thrown away. Same-day response is available when a crew is available, and after-hours calls are expected.

What cleanup includes

Debris and wet contents

Soaked boxes, books, particleboard and padding come out so they stop wicking moisture.

Material removal

Wet drywall, carpet pad, insulation and trim that cannot be dried are removed and bagged.

Cleaning and sanitizing

Surfaces and framing are cleaned, and antimicrobial treatment is used where the water category calls for it.

Odor control

Odor is removed at the source. Deodorizing only masks it if damp material is still in place.

Contents get their own triage. Solid wood furniture and hard goods can usually be cleaned. Upholstered pieces that sat in gray or black water, and paper goods, often cannot. Photograph everything before it goes, since your insurer may want to see it.

Cleanup by water category

Water categoryTypical sourceWhat cleanup usually means
Category 1Broken supply line, tub overflow with clean waterRemove soaked porous materials, dry, and clean surfaces
Category 2Washing machine or dishwasher dischargeRemove porous materials, clean and sanitize, treat framing
Category 3Sewage, rising groundwater, floodwaterFull removal of affected porous materials, cleaning, sanitizing, containment where needed

These categories come from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and water can move from one category to the next the longer it sits. Ask any company you call whether its crews work to S500.

Mud and storm cleanup in Draper

Draper has had mud events as well as pipe breaks. In June 2009, mud from the Corner Canyon burn scar put 2 to 3 feet of water and mud into three homes near 12600 South and 2100 East, and after the August 3, 2023 monsoon storm, homes were reported filled with mud and debris. Mud brings silt into every crack, which is why storm cleanup takes longer than a clean-water leak.

Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood losses, including post-wildfire mudflows, according to the Utah Insurance Department. Flood coverage is a separate policy, and the department notes a standard 30-day waiting period before NFIP coverage takes effect. If you are unsure which applies, ask your insurer before cleanup starts and keep photos. Basement and ground-level flooding is covered in more detail on flood damage restoration.

What shapes the cost of cleanup

Water category

Category 3 water means more removal, more protection for workers and more disposal.

Area and contents

The more rooms and the more belongings affected, the more labor and handling.

Materials

Porous materials come out. Concrete, tile and solid wood can usually be cleaned in place.

Odor history

Water that sat for days leaves odor in framing, which may need extra treatment.

Access and disposal

Basement stairs, long carries and hauling add time.

Common questions

Do you handle water damage cleanup emergencies at night or on weekends in Draper?

Yes. Dispatch answers 24/7 and after-hours calls are normal for this work. Same-day response is available when a crew is available, and we never promise a specific arrival time.

Is cleanup different from mitigation?

Yes. Mitigation stops the damage and dries the structure, while cleanup removes ruined materials and sanitizes. On many jobs the two run together.

Should I throw away wet items before you arrive?

Photograph them first, then set aside what is obviously ruined. Do not discard anything your insurer may want to inspect unless it is a health risk, and ask the insurer if you are unsure.

Can wet drywall be saved?

Sometimes, if it is clean-source water, the wall is still firm, and it is dried fast. Drywall that is sagging, soft, stained through or soaked by gray or black water is normally removed.

What if I smell something after cleanup?

A lingering smell usually means a damp material or a sealed cavity still holds moisture. We use moisture meters to find it rather than adding fragrance.
