Bellevue in Draper and why basements matter here

Bellevue is a neighborhood in Draper that FOX 13, KUTV and KJZZ all referred to by name in their January 2023 coverage of flooded homes. KJZZ placed it near 13300 South and 400 East, and KUTV named the Cherry Crest Drive area. That is how news coverage located it.

Listing sites give a rough picture of the housing. One sampled page, bestutahrealestate.com, showed 48 listings, most built between 2000 and 2009 and the rest between 2010 and 2019, with a median near 4,811 square feet on about a quarter acre. That is listing-site evidence, not official data.

What the January 2023 groundwater event told homeowners

On January 10 and 11, 2023, rain fell on wet snow and groundwater flooding followed. FOX 13 reported damage to over 30 homes, and KSL reported about 30. KSL quoted the mayor saying some subdivisions have private drain systems built for groundwater and that the city's storm drains worked.

Reports at the time described groundwater in private drain systems, and responsibility was disputed. KUTV also relayed Salt Lake County's statement that it did not believe Corner Canyon Creek was the source, which is one reason the Corner Canyon area comes up in the same conversation.

The Utah Geological Survey says shallow groundwater can flood basements and other low-lying structures. In May 2023 a UGS geologist told KJZZ that groundwater reports along the Wasatch Front included Draper. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status has not been verified.

Loss scenarios in large Bellevue-style homes

These scenarios can happen in homes of this type. None is a claim about local frequency.

  • Seepage or rising water in a below-grade room.
  • A power outage that stops a sump pump while water is still coming in.
  • A large finished basement where carpet, pad and lower drywall wet from the bottom up.
  • A slow-drying lower level where delayed drying lets mold risk build.

Bellevue water safety before we arrive

Stop what you can

If the water comes from a broken supply line, shut the main valve. Groundwater cannot be shut off.

Respect the electrical hazard

Stay out of standing water if outlets, cords or a panel may be wet. Have the power cut off from a dry location or by an electrician.

Gas smell means leave

If you smell gas, go outside and call the gas utility from there. Do not switch anything on or off inside.

Avoid sewage water

If the water looks or smells like sewage, keep family and pets out and wait for the crew.

If your home sits on a private drain system kept up by an HOA, tell the HOA the day it happens and keep records. Dispatch is at (385) 988-9410.

Extraction and drying in a deep lower level

1

Moisture mapping

A technician scans with a thermal imaging camera and confirms with moisture meters, wall by wall and across the slab edges.

2

Water extraction

Submersible pumps handle standing water, then portable or truck-mounted extraction units pull water from carpet and pad.

3

Drying setup

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers go in, and the lower level is closed off as one drying zone.

4

Daily readings

We check the meters every day, adjust equipment and remove it only when readings match a dry reference.

EPA guidance says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, so drying plans should begin as soon as water is found. Documenting a groundwater or sump loss for your insurer

Take photos and video before anything is moved, write a list of damaged items, and note the date and time you found the water. Then ask your insurer what your policy covers.

The Utah Geological Survey notes that standard homeowners insurance does not cover groundwater flooding, and flood is often excluded too. Ask your insurer whether you carry an endorsement such as water backup or sump overflow. We cannot promise coverage, and the insurer makes that decision.

Common questions

Do you handle water damage in Bellevue at night or on weekends, and is a crew available?

Dispatch answers by phone at all hours, and same-day response is available when a crew is available. We never promise a specific arrival time.

Does a sump pump failure count as a water damage call?

Yes. If a sump pit overflowed or the pump stopped, water can spread through carpet and lower walls. We extract, map moisture and dry the space, but the pump itself is a plumber's or installer's job.

Should I call my HOA about a drain system?

If your home is on a private drain system maintained by an HOA, tell them the day the problem happens and keep records of the call. We do not decide who is responsible.

Will my homeowners policy pay for groundwater?

Standard homeowners policies often exclude groundwater and flood. Ask your insurer about endorsements, and do not rely on us for a coverage answer.
