When standing water is an emergency

Standing water is an emergency when it is spreading, rising, or soaking into anything you cannot easily replace. A burst pipe in a kitchen, a water heater that let go, a failed washing machine hose, or a basement that took on water overnight all qualify. Emergency water removal in Draper means a crew with pumps and extraction units, available around the clock.

Dispatch is open 24/7, including holidays, and same-day response is available when a crew is available. We do not promise a specific arrival time, because honest scheduling beats a number that slips. What we do is tell you plainly what to do until we get there.

Stop the water first

Find the shutoff

The main water valve is usually where the line enters the house, near the water heater or the meter. Turn it off before anything else.

Handle power safely

Power off to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. If water touches the panel or outlets, call the utility or 911 first.

Protect valuables

Lift electronics, papers, rugs and furniture legs off wet floors. Keep people and pets out of any water that may be contaminated.

Take photos

Photograph the source, the water line on walls and damaged items. Your insurer will want to see them.

How extraction works

Pumps handle depth. A submersible pump clears inches or feet of water quickly, then truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull water out of carpet, pad and low spots that a pump cannot reach. Wet vacs help in small areas but cannot keep up with a flooded basement.

Extraction is the start, not the finish. Once standing water is gone, the structure still holds moisture, so drying equipment goes in next, which is covered on the water mitigation page. Skipping that step is how a dry-looking floor turns into a moldy wall a few weeks later.

Burst pipe water damage

Burst pipes are the most common reason people call us at night. A supply line fails behind a wall, a fitting separates under a sink, or a frozen pipe splits and thaws into a flood. Supply lines are under pressure, so a break can discharge a lot of water in minutes.

The sequence is the same every time. Shut the water off at the main, open a faucet at the lowest point to drain the line, then call for extraction before the water travels into walls, ceilings and floors below. If a leak is slow, such as a weeping fitting under a vanity, the damage is quieter but the drywall and cabinet base can be soaked for weeks, and the water damage repair page covers what that rebuild looks like.

Draper has added a seasonal wrinkle. Pressurized irrigation is supplied by WaterPro and Draper Irrigation Co., not the city, and it typically turns on in mid-April, so a startup can reveal a buried line break that fills a window well or crawl space. Know where your own irrigation shutoff is before spring.

The standard behind the work

The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration is the reference many professionals work from, and you can ask any company whether its crews follow it. It sorts water into clean, gray and black categories, and that category decides what can be dried and what has to be removed.

Common questions

Do you handle emergency water removal at night or on weekends in Draper?

Yes. Dispatch is open 24/7, including weekends and holidays. Same-day response is available when a crew is available, and we never promise a specific arrival time.

Can a wet vac handle a flooded basement?

A wet vac works for a puddle but cannot keep up with inches of standing water, and it does not remove water from pad and subfloor. Pumps and extraction units do.

What if the water came from a toilet or drain?

Stay out and keep children and pets away. Water from a drain is contaminated and needs containment and cleaning, which our flood damage restoration page explains.

Is extraction enough, or do I need drying too?

Extraction removes standing water, but wall cavities and subfloors stay wet. Drying with air movers and dehumidifiers follows, and moisture readings confirm it worked.

Will insurance cover emergency water removal?

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, and gradual leaks often are not. Flood damage is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer.
