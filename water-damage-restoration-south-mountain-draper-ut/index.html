South Mountain: gated streets and shared walls

South Mountain is the name used for a residential area of Draper made up of gated, HOA-managed subdivisions arranged around a golf course. Homes.com describes it as subdivisions of gated communities with HOAs, and Redfin keeps a South Mountain neighborhood page for Draper.

Exact edges are not published, so confirm your address with Draper City. The name is also a landform label, and some listing-site web addresses file South Mountain homes under Sandy. One listing site puts SunCrest about 1.8 miles from its South Mountain map point, so see our SunCrest page if you live toward that side.

What the listing data suggests about the homes here

Listing sites sampled show a mixed stock. Redfin lists single-family homes, townhomes and condos. A sample of 275 listings on bestutahrealestate.com had 167 built from 2000 to 2009 and 78 from 1990 to 1999, with none earlier than 1990. That is label evidence, not official data.

Built between 1990 and 2009 means many of these homes are now roughly 17 to 36 years old. Water heaters, supply lines and appliance hoses wear out on their own schedules, and homes in that bracket can be reaching those points. The median was about 3,585 square feet on about 0.18 acre.

Weather and ground water still apply. The Utah Geological Survey says shallow groundwater can flood basements, and that standard homeowners insurance does not cover groundwater flooding. A groundwater map specific to South Mountain has not been verified. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status has not been verified.

In August 2023, KSL reported about 2 inches of rain in an hour and a state of emergency declared by Draper's mayor. That coverage named other Draper locations, not South Mountain.

Loss scenarios in townhomes and larger family houses

  • Shared-wall leaks. In an HOA townhome or condo, a failed supply line can push water into the unit next door. Tell your HOA and your neighbor early. Who pays depends on the governing documents and the insurers, so ask them.
  • Water heater failure. A tank in a basement closet or garage can empty onto finished flooring. The Utah earthquake program calls strapping a water heater one of the best non-structural measures, and warns a damaged unit can cause flooding. Read more on water heater leak cleanup.
  • Upstairs bathrooms over living space. Water can drop through ceilings into rooms below and sit behind drywall.
  • Finished basements. Window well or irrigation overflow in storms can reach carpet and wall bottoms.
  • Appliance hoses. See appliance leak water damage.

Safety first before you let anyone in

Stop the water

Close the shutoff valve for the fixture, or the main valve if you cannot find it. Townhome owners may need the HOA for a shared main.

Cut electrical risk

Stay out of rooms where water reaches outlets, cords or appliances. Turn off breakers only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.

Gas and sewage

If you smell gas, leave and call the gas utility from outside. If the water is sewage, stay out of it and keep children and pets away.

Notify and document

Tell the HOA and any affected neighbor. Photograph everything, then call (385) 988-9410.

Have your gate or call box details ready when you phone.

Insurance documentation for HOA and homeowner claims

Photograph the source and every wet surface. Write a list of damaged items and the date you found the water. Then phone your insurer and ask what the policy covers.

The HOA may carry a master policy for shared structure, and the split with your own policy depends on your governing documents. We cannot promise coverage. Standard homeowners policies often exclude flood and groundwater, so ask your insurer about both before assuming either way.
