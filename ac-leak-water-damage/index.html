Where air conditioner water comes from

A cooling system pulls moisture out of the air, and that water has to go somewhere. It drips off the evaporator coil into a pan and leaves through a condensate drain line. When the line clogs with sludge or the pan rusts through, the water backs up and spills into whatever sits below.

Air handlers often sit in attics, furnace closets or basement mechanical rooms, so the spill soaks insulation or a ceiling. A coil that iced over can add a second surprise: it thaws all at once and sends more water than the drain can carry. The EPA advises keeping drip pans clean and drain lines unobstructed and flowing properly.

Summer is when this matters. The National Weather Service's Salt Lake City normals put July's average high at 94.0 degrees F, with August at 91.7 and June at 84.1. Systems that run hard for months have a lot of condensate to move.

AC leak safety steps before the drywall drops

Turn the system off

Use the thermostat first, then the unit's service switch. Running it keeps making condensate.

Mind the electricity

Air handlers and attic wiring are live. If water is dripping from a light fixture, switch off that circuit at a dry panel.

Call both trades

An HVAC technician fixes the drain or coil. Call (385) 988-9410 for extraction and drying.

Protect and photograph

Move items out from under the stain, put a bucket in place and take dated photos.

If the ceiling bulges, stay out from under it. Water trapped above drywall can drop without warning.

How an AC leak gets dried and rebuilt

1

Find the extent

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera trace water through ceiling drywall, insulation and wall tops.

2

Remove wet material

Saturated insulation and soft drywall come out so framing can dry.

3

Dry the cavity

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until readings match dry areas, with ANSI/IICRC S500 as a reference many professionals work from.

4

Treat and clean

Antimicrobial treatment goes on affected surfaces, and HEPA air scrubbers run if material was removed.

5

Rebuild

New insulation, drywall, texture and paint finish the interior scope.

We do not service air conditioners. The technician fixes the drain, pan or coil and we handle the water damage. The EPA suggests holding indoor humidity below 60 percent, ideally between 30 and 50, which is the range dehumidifiers work toward.

Condensate leaks and your homeowners policy

Insurers often separate sudden overflow from damage that built up slowly. A drain line that clogs and spills one afternoon reads differently from a pan that dripped for months. Ask whether your policy treats maintenance-related leaks differently and how the adjuster wants moisture documented. Coverage is never something we can promise ahead of the adjuster.

Keeping a condensate line clear

  • Have a technician flush the drain line before cooling season.
  • Check the pan for standing water or rust whenever you change the filter.
  • Replace filters on schedule, since a dirty filter helps a coil ice over.
  • Consider a float switch that shuts the system off when the pan fills.

Common questions

Is AC leak water damage help available in Draper at night or on weekends?

Yes. Dispatch answers 24/7 and after-hours calls are routine. Same-day response is available when a crew is available, but we do not promise an arrival time.

Do you repair the air conditioner itself?

No. An HVAC technician repairs the drain, pan or coil. We dry and rebuild the damaged interior.

Is a swamp cooler leak handled the same way?

The drying is the same. The source differs, because a cooler's supply line, float or pad leaks, and the owner or a technician fixes that.

Will the stain go away with paint?

Not reliably. If the drywall behind it is still wet, the stain returns and mold can start.
