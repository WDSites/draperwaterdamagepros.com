SunCrest on Traverse Ridge and why a leak matters here

SunCrest is a master-planned community on Traverse Ridge. SunCrest sits near the line between Draper, Highland and Alpine, so confirm your address before relying on a city service. Draper's website posted a June 2026 ordinance adjusting the Draper-Highland boundary at a Suncrest Drive address, and listing sites tag some homes as Utah County. We draw no boundaries here.

An older KSL article describes about 3,800 acres, half of it open space, begun in 1999 and reached by an access road of about 8 miles to Utah County. Listing sites sampled show most homes built from 2000 to 2009, with a median size near 3,570 square feet. A big house means many rooms below any failure.

What the public record says about weather, hazards and water service

The National Weather Service normals for Salt Lake City put the January average low at 24.2 degrees F. Ridge-top conditions are not in that station's data, so treat it as a rough guide for pipes in unheated spaces.

Draper's December 2025 hazard mitigation annex names the Corner Canyon and Suncrest foothills among wildfire-exposed areas. That is separate from a plumbing leak, and we are not saying anything in SunCrest has burned or flooded. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status has not been verified.

We have not verified which water utility serves SunCrest. Draper City says it runs one of two city water systems and WaterPro/Draper Irrigation runs the other. Check your bill, then call Draper City Water at (801) 831-7212 or WaterPro/Draper Irrigation at (801) 571-2232 after hours.

Loss scenarios in larger ridge-top houses

  • A supply line in an unheated space can freeze in a cold snap, then split at the thaw. See our frozen pipe page.
  • In a large house, a burst pipe upstairs can soak ceilings, walls and floors on the way down.
  • Wind-driven storms can push water past flashing, and our roof leak page covers what that looks like inside.
  • A water heater in a finished basement can often leak for days before anyone notices.

These are patterns that can happen in homes like these, not local statistics. KSL describes an access road of about 8 miles, which is one more reason to phone early.

Water safety steps in a SunCrest home

Stop the water

Close the main shutoff or the valve nearest the failed fixture.

Avoid live power

Stay out of rooms where water reaches outlets or the panel.

Sewage means stay out

Water from a drain is contaminated. Keep children and pets away.

Gas smell, leave

Walk outside and call the gas utility from there.

Once everyone is safe, call (385) 988-9410. Early calls help in a community reached by a long access road.

Extraction and drying across several floors

1

Moisture mapping

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show how far water traveled between floors, behind drywall and under flooring.

2

Extraction

Submersible pumps and portable or truck-mounted extraction units remove standing water and soaked carpet pad.

3

Drying setup

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are placed by room, with poly containment so wet areas dry apart from dry ones.

4

Daily readings

We return to log moisture readings and adjust equipment until materials reach their dry targets.

5

Cleanup and treatment

Antimicrobial treatment goes on surfaces that stayed wet, and we list the interior repair scope.

Tall rooms and open stairwells hold a lot of air, so dehumidifier placement matters, as the structural drying page explains. ANSI/IICRC S500 is a reference many professionals work from, and EPA guidance says to dry water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours.

Records to keep for the insurance call

  • Take dated photos and video of every wet room before anything is moved.
  • Write a list of damaged flooring, furniture and belongings.
  • Note when you saw the water and when it was stopped.
  • Call your insurer and ask what your policy covers.

We cannot promise coverage. Standard homeowners policies often exclude flood and groundwater, so ask your insurer how it treats your source.
