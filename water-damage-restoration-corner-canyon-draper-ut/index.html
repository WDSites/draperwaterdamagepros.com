What people mean by Corner Canyon

Corner Canyon here means the area on Draper's east foothills. KSL in 2009 and 2023 uses phrases like the Corner Canyon area and the Corner Canyon section of Draper. The name also belongs to a high school, trails and open space, so it is not only a residential label.

Residential edges of the name are not published, so confirm your address when you call. Deseret News reported in 2019 that Draper and Salt Lake County preserved about 4,500 acres across Corner Canyon and Traverse Ridge, running toward the borders of Sandy.

Dated water events tied to the Corner Canyon name

These come from news reports and city documents, and each is a past event, not a prediction. Draper's hazard mitigation annex records an August 8, 2008 wildfire in Corner Canyon of about 680 acres, with no homes impacted by the fire itself.

KSL reported a June 17, 2009 flash flood near 12600 South and 2100 East. Three homes took 2 to 3 feet of water and mud under a National Weather Service flash flood warning.

KSL also reported that a hillside landslide on April 22, 2023 took two homes in the Corner Canyon area, with no injuries. On August 3, 2023, a cloudburst sent mud behind homes above Highland Drive at 13800 South, according to KMYU.

How water usually gets into a foothill home

Housing here varies, so we do not assume one construction type. Water on a slope behaves differently than water on flat ground. After a hard storm it can run along the lot line or push through a low door threshold.

  • Runoff and mud reaching a walkout door or garage after a cloudburst.
  • Window wells that fill faster than they drain, then spill into a lower level.
  • Hillside drainage that stops carrying water away when packed with silt.
  • Basement carpet, pad and drywall wicking water up after a storm.

Silt carries fine soil into flooring and cavities, so cleanup is more than pumping. For heavy storm intrusion, read our storm water damage page.

Water safety before we arrive

Stop the source

Shut off the main valve if a pipe or appliance is leaking. If water comes in from outside, stay out of the lowest rooms.

Power

Do not step into standing water near outlets, cords or a panel. Call an electrician if water reached them.

Sewage and gas

Stay out of backed-up drain water. A gas smell means everyone leaves first, and the gas utility gets the call from outside.

Mud and slopes

Do not walk behind the house on soft, moving ground. A fresh slide can shift again.

Once it is safe, take photos before you move anything, then call (385) 988-9410.

Extraction, mud removal and drying

The method depends on how much water came in and what it touched, so we do not quote a price or timeline up front.

1

Moisture mapping

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera find wet drywall, subfloor and framing, including areas that look dry.

2

Extraction

Submersible pumps and portable or truck-mounted extraction units pull out standing water and slurry.

3

Drying setup

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers go in, and material that cannot dry in place, such as soaked pad, comes out.

4

Daily readings

We check moisture each day and adjust equipment until readings show the structure has dried.

EPA guidance says to dry water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth. Our flood damage restoration and water damage cleanup pages cover larger jobs.

Documenting the loss for your insurer

Take wide and close photos of every wet room and the waterline on walls. Write a list of damaged belongings, note the date the damage started, and call your insurer early. Ask what your policy covers, because we cannot promise coverage.

Standard homeowners policies often exclude flood and groundwater, so ask how runoff, mud or a drain backup is classified. The hazard mitigation annex describes a Special Flood Hazard Area along Corner Canyon Creek downstream of I-15, which is not a statement about homes in the foothills. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status has not been verified.
