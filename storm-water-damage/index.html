How storm water gets inside a Draper home

Most storm water never rises like a flood. It arrives through weak points at the building envelope. Gutters overflow and dump water beside the foundation. Window wells fill faster than they drain. Wind drives rain sideways under siding and around window frames, and water sheets across a driveway toward a garage door.

Draper's August 3, 2023 monsoon storm shows how fast it can happen. About 2 inches of rain fell in a little over an hour, and a state of emergency followed. Intense bursts like that overwhelm drains built for ordinary rain, so water goes looking for the lowest opening.

That is the line between this page and our flood damage restoration page. Storm water here means rain that reaches the building and the grade around it. A rising creek or canal belongs in the flood category, which insurers treat differently.

Storm safety steps before you touch anything

Stop the inflow

Only if it is safe and the rain has eased, redirect water away from the opening with sandbags or boards. Never wade into deep water to do it.

Respect the electricity

Stay out of a basement with standing water if outlets or the panel could be wet. Call the utility or 911 if water has reached them.

Call for help

Dial (385) 988-9410 for pumping and drying. Do not drive through flooded roads to reach the house.

Photograph first

Take dated photos of the water line, the entry point and the gutters before cleanup begins.

Storm water often carries mud, fertilizer and debris. Wear boots and gloves, and keep children and pets out of it.

Cleanup order after a storm: mud out, water out, then dry

1

Remove debris and silt

Mud and gravel come out first so pumps and extractors do not clog and the floor can be inspected.

2

Pump and extract

Submersible pumps clear standing water. Portable and truck-mounted extraction units pull the rest from carpet and slab.

3

Map the moisture

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show where water went into walls and under flooring.

4

Dry the structure

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until readings match an unaffected area, following ANSI/IICRC S500 as a reference many professionals work from.

5

Clean and treat

Hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized, antimicrobial is applied and HEPA air scrubbers run where material was removed.

Mud first is not optional. Silt left in place keeps materials wet and makes later drying harder. Our water damage cleanup page covers debris removal and sanitizing in more detail.

What changes the cost of storm water cleanup

Amount of silt

Heavy mud takes more labor to shovel and haul before extraction can start.

Water depth

Inches of water on a slab is a different job than a flooded finished basement.

Flooring type

Carpet and pad usually come out. Tile and sealed concrete often stay.

Affected levels

Water that reached an upstairs wall adds work beyond the basement.

Hours since entry

Waiting lets moisture soak into drywall and framing.

Contamination

Water that picked up sewage or heavy debris needs added containment and sanitizing.

Storm water, flood and your policy

Coverage often turns on how the water arrived. FEMA says most homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage and that a National Flood Insurance Program policy typically takes 30 days to take effect. That means a policy bought as storm clouds gather will not help.

Ask your insurer whether wind-driven rain, a clogged gutter and a surface flood are treated as separate causes. We cannot promise coverage, but we keep moisture logs and photos that an adjuster can read.

Before monsoon season: gutters, wells and grading

  • Clear gutters and extend downspouts several feet from the foundation.
  • Clean window wells and check that their drains are not packed with soil.
  • Walk the lot and look for low spots where the ground slopes toward the house.
  • Keep the basement floor drain clear and test any sump pump.

Sandbags, basins and who handles which water

Salt Lake County supplies sandbags, 25 per vehicle per day, and says residents are responsible for protecting their own property. Draper City's storm water division runs more than 100 detention and retention basins, but those cannot stop water from reaching a window well on your lot. County Flood Control handles rivers, creeks and canals, while local roads fall to the municipality.

Common questions

Is emergency storm water damage cleanup available in Draper?

Yes. Dispatch answers 24/7, including during storms. A crew can be sent the same day when one is available, though we do not promise an arrival time.

Is storm water damage the same as flood damage?

Not exactly. Storm water here means rain reaching the building and grade. A rising creek or canal is a flood, and insurers handle the two differently.

Should I save mud-soaked carpet?

Usually not. Mud-laden water counts as contaminated, and carpet and pad are porous. Hard surfaces are more often cleaned and kept.

Can you stop water from coming in again?

No. Grading, window wells and drainage are separate trades. We dry and repair what the water damaged.
