A small old core inside a growing city

Historic Draper is the phrase Draper City's preservation pages use for the older core around Fort Street and 13200 South. Boundaries here are approximate, and the city itself says its historic resources are found on only a few small streets. If you live nearby and are unsure whether this page fits your address, call (385) 988-9410 and dispatch will sort it out.

The city's walking tour booklet covers about a dozen homes along Fort Street and 13200 South, with dates from the 1850s to about 1900. Housing statistics for the area have not been verified, so we write about older homes in general, including the historic ones on the tour.

What we know about water in this part of town

Draper has two water systems. According to Draper City's utilities page, the city runs one and WaterPro/Draper Irrigation runs the other. After hours, Draper City Water takes calls at (801) 831-7212 and WaterPro customers use (801) 571-2232.

Winter matters for old plumbing. NWS Salt Lake City climate normals show 21 days in a typical year when the daytime high never climbs past 32 degrees F. Any pipe in an uninsulated wall, cellar or crawl space is exposed on those days. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status has not been verified.

How water tends to hurt a house with some age on it

Older homes often carry supply lines and drains past their first design life. Pipes of that era can include cast iron or galvanized steel, which corrode from the inside and may weep at joints for a long time before failing.

  • Plaster and lath ceilings can sag or stain when a bathroom above leaks, and wet plaster is heavy.
  • Solid wood floors can cup or buckle and dry slowly. Our carpet water damage page covers what sits on top.
  • Cellars and crawl spaces can collect seepage. See crawl space water damage.
  • Winter bursts can follow a cold snap. See burst pipe water damage.

Historic Draper safety steps before anyone starts work

Stop the water

Close the main valve inside. If it will not turn, call the utility numbers above for a street shutoff.

Mind the power

Stay off wet floors near outlets and cords. Shut off breakers only if the panel is dry.

Gas smell

Leave, then call the gas utility or 911 from outside.

Sewage

Stay out and keep children and pets away.

Age adds two cautions. The EPA says homes built before 1978 may have lead paint, and its renovation, repair and painting rule covers paid work that disturbs painted surfaces there. It also says materials in older buildings can contain asbestos and should be tested before disturbance. Please do not sand or tear out suspect material yourself.

Extraction and drying, one step at a time

1

Moisture mapping

Meter readings and a thermal imaging camera find wet spots, so plaster stays closed where possible.

2

Extraction

Submersible pumps and extraction units remove standing water first.

3

Drying setup

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers go in, with gentle airflow on solid wood and plaster.

4

Daily readings

We log readings daily and adjust equipment until the structure is dry.

If a wall may hold lead paint or asbestos, we can start drying while the material is tested. We do not test or abate it. The EPA advises drying within 24 to 48 hours. More on the method is on our structural drying page.

We handle water damage, not preservation work. Historic woodwork and original finishes may need a specialist after drying, and Draper City's historic preservation page is a place to start.

Writing it down for your insurer

Before anything is moved, take photos and video of every wet room. Make a written list of damaged items, note the date you found the water, and keep damaged materials until the adjuster has seen them. Then call your insurer and ask what your policy covers. We cannot promise coverage.

Standard homeowners policies often exclude flood and groundwater, while a sudden pipe failure is treated differently. Describe what happened plainly and ask which category applies.

Can plaster walls be dried without tearing them out?

Often we can try. Readings and a thermal camera show where the wet spots are, and slow drying may save plaster. Soaked sections can still have to come out.

Tell us when you call. Ask about lead-safe practices before any sanding or demolition.
