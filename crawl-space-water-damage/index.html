Why wet crawl spaces turn up in only some Draper homes

Full basements are the usual foundation here, which makes crawl spaces the exception. You find them under older homes, additions, porches and bump-outs. We say that plainly because a basement flood needs a different page, and our flood damage restoration page covers it.

Real local causes are modest. A sprinkler line that breaks at spring startup can pour water beside the foundation. A leaking supply or drain line drips onto bare ground for weeks. Snowmelt runs along a poorly graded yard and into a vent or access hatch.

The Utah Geological Survey notes that shallow groundwater can flood basements and other low-lying structures. A crawl space sits even lower in the building, so high groundwater in a wet spring can show up there first.

Crawl space safety steps and confined space cautions

Stop the supply

Shut off the main or the irrigation valve if a line is the source. Do not crawl in to find the leak.

Stay out of the space

Crawl spaces are tight, wet and dark. Wiring may run through standing water, and you cannot get out quickly.

Call for crews

Call (385) 988-9410 for pumping and drying, and a plumber or irrigation technician for the line.

Document from the hatch

Take dated photos from the opening and note when you first saw water.

Gas lines and electrical cables are often routed under floors. A gas smell means everyone leaves the house first, and the gas utility gets the call from outside.

Pumping and drying the space under the floor

1

Inspect and map

Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera check joists, subfloor and the rooms above.

2

Pump out the water

Submersible pumps clear standing water through the hatch or a vent opening.

3

Remove wet insulation

Saturated batts come out and are bagged, because wet insulation keeps the subfloor damp.

4

Dry in place

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run until readings match an unaffected area.

5

Treat and recheck

Antimicrobial goes on exposed framing, HEPA air scrubbers run if material came out, and readings continue.

Dirt floors keep releasing moisture long after the pump stops. That is why we measure before removing equipment and follow ANSI/IICRC S500 as a reference many professionals work from. We do not install vapor barriers or grade soil.

Crawl space water and insurance

Insurers often separate a sudden line failure from groundwater or seepage. The Utah Geological Survey states that standard homeowners insurance does not cover groundwater flooding, so the cause of the water matters. Ask your insurer how it treats a broken sprinkler line and long-term moisture. We make no promise about what a claim pays.

Mistakes to avoid after crawl space water

  • Going in to look without anyone knowing where you are.
  • Leaving wet insulation up because the rooms feel fine.
  • Sealing the vents so water cannot get out.
  • Skipping dehumidification once the pump quits.

Radon, vapor barriers and a damp crawl space

The Utah Department of Environmental Quality says one in three Utah homes test above the EPA action level of 4 picocuries per liter. The EPA calls radon responsible for about 21,000 lung cancer deaths a year. Wet soil and open ground can raise the question, so consider testing.

The EPA's radon-resistant construction basics call for 6 mil polyethylene sheeting over a gravel layer and sealed foundation openings. That is guidance for new building, but it shows why a vapor barrier matters. Our mold remediation page covers growth. Basements in Alpine and Herriman are more common than crawl spaces.
